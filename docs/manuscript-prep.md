# Manuscript Prep

Your manuscript is your book's text — the words your readers will read. Preparing it well is the most important step. A clean, well-formatted manuscript makes the rest of publishing easier and makes your book look professional.

This page assumes you have already written and edited your book. It focuses on getting the file ready for KDP.

## Step 1: Finish writing and editing

Before you format, make sure your manuscript is complete:

- The story or content is finished.
- You have proofread for spelling, grammar, and typos.
- Ideally, someone else has read it too — a friend, family member, or beta reader.
- All chapters are in the right order.

If you used AI to help write or edit, review every page yourself. You are the author, and you are responsible for what is in the book.

## Step 2: Use a clean word processor file

KDP accepts several file types. The most common and easiest to work with is **Microsoft Word (.docx)**. You can also use:

- `.doc` (older Word format)
- `.rtf`
- `.html`
- `.epub` (mostly for eBooks)

**Tip:** Use Word, Google Docs (export to .docx), or LibreOffice Writer (save as .docx). Avoid weird formatting like text boxes, multiple columns, or floating images unless you know what you're doing.

## Step 3: Set up basic formatting

Here is a simple, KDP-friendly setup:

- **Font:** Use a standard, readable font like Times New Roman, Georgia, or Calibri. 11 or 12 point for body text.
- **Line spacing:** 1.15 or 1.5 line spacing is comfortable to read.
- **Margins:** 1 inch (2.54 cm) on all sides is a safe starting point.
- **Paragraph indent:** Indent the first line of each paragraph by 0.25 or 0.3 inches, OR put a blank line between paragraphs — but not both.
- **Chapter headings:** Use Heading styles (Heading 1, Heading 2) so KDP can detect your chapters and build a table of contents.

**Do not** manually hit Enter many times to force a page break. Use Insert → Page Break (or Ctrl+Enter / Cmd+Return) to start a new chapter on a new page.

## Step 4: Add front matter

Front matter is what comes before your main story or content. Include at minimum:

- **Title page:** Your book title, subtitle, and your name as the author.
- **Copyright page:** A simple statement like "Copyright (c) 2026 Your Name. All rights reserved." You can add more later.

Optional front matter: dedication, table of contents, about the author, other books by you.

## Step 5: Add back matter

Back matter comes after your main content. Consider including:

- **About the author:** A short bio and a photo if you like.
- **Other books:** A list of your other books with links.
- **Call to action:** Ask readers to leave a review on Amazon.

## Step 6: Save a copy for print and one for eBook

Print books and eBooks format differently:

- **Print:** Fixed page size, margins, and page numbers. Headers/footers with page numbers work well.
- **eBook:** Reflowable text. Readers change font size, so page numbers do not exist. Avoid fixed page breaks where possible.

You can use the same .docx for both, but many authors create two versions to get the best result.

## Step 7: Check your file before uploading

- Open the file and scroll through every page.
- Make sure chapter headings look right.
- Make sure images (if any) are clear and not stretched.
- Turn on formatting marks (¶) in Word to spot extra spaces or stray page breaks.
- Save a PDF to preview how pages will look.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Using the spacebar or many Enter key presses to center or position text. Use alignment and page breaks instead.
- Pasting text from a website with hidden formatting. Paste into a plain text editor first, then into Word.
- Forgetting to remove "Track Changes" or comments. Accept or reject all changes and delete comments before uploading.
- Using a font that is not embedded or uncommon. Stick to standard fonts.

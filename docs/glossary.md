# Glossary

Plain-English definitions for terms used in this guide. If you see a word that is not here, let us know and we will add it.

**Amazon KDP** — Kindle Direct Publishing, Amazon's self-publishing platform. You can publish paperback, hardcover, and Kindle eBooks through it.

**ASIN** — Amazon Standard Identification Number. Amazon's own identifier for Kindle eBooks and products. You do not need to buy one.

**Beta reader** — A person who reads your book before publication and gives you feedback.

**Bleed** — When images or color run to the edge of a printed page, with no white margin. Requires special formatting.

**Blurb** — Your book description, usually on the back cover and product page.

**eBook** — A digital book read on Kindle devices and apps.

**Front matter** — Pages at the beginning of a book, such as the title page and copyright page.

**Hardcover** — A print book with a rigid cover.

**Imprint** — A brand or publisher name under which you publish (for example, Asdale Dynamics).

**ISBN** — International Standard Book Number, a unique identifier for a book.

**KDP Select** — An optional program that makes your eBook exclusive to Amazon for 90 days in exchange for promotional tools.

**Manuscript** — Your book's text file before it becomes a published book.

**Paperback** — A print book with a flexible soft cover.

**Print-on-demand** — A system where books are printed only when someone orders them. No inventory to store.

**Proof copy** — A single copy of your print book that you order to check for errors before selling it.

**Royalty** — Your share of each book sale.

**Spine** — The part of a print book's cover that shows on a bookshelf.

**Subtitle** — A secondary title that explains what the book is about.

**Table of contents** — A list of chapters and their page numbers.

**Thumbnails** — The small version of your book cover shown in search results and on product pages.

**Trim size** — The finished width and height of a print book.

**Widow / orphan** — A single line of a paragraph left alone at the top or bottom of a page. Generally avoided in professional typesetting.
